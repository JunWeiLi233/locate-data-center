Where Should America’s Next Sustainable Data Center Be Built?

The Challenge
AI is reshaping the world—and creating unprecedented demand for data center infrastructure.
As AI workloads accelerate, the world needs to build more digital infrastructure. But where and how we build that infrastructure matters.
Data centers consume significant amounts of electricity, water, land, materials, and other resources. A location that looks attractive today may create decades of environmental, operational, and economic challenges.
Where should we build the next generation of AI infrastructure—and what makes a location truly future-ready?
Your mission is to create a decision engine and use data, technology, and systems thinking to identify the optimal geographic location for a next-generation sustainable AI data center, balancing environmental impact, infrastructure availability, resilience, and long-term economic viability.
Inspired by the principles of open, efficient, scalable infrastructure, teams will explore how smarter location decisions can help accelerate a more sustainable digital future.
The Problem
The location of a data center creates a complex interaction between energy, water, climate, infrastructure, communities, and the broader industrial ecosystem.
Teams should consider factors such as:
Energy & Carbon — Grid carbon intensity, renewable energy availability, clean-energy penetration, and future energy capacity
Heat Recovery & Reuse — Opportunities to capture and reuse waste heat from the data center for district heating, greenhouses, industrial processes, agriculture, aquaculture, or other beneficial applications
Water — Water availability, scarcity, cooling requirements, and long-term water risk.  Consider water used in the generation of grid power as well.
Climate Resilience — Exposure to extreme heat, flooding, hurricanes, drought, wildfires, and other climate risks
Grid & Infrastructure — Grid reliability, transmission capacity, energy costs, connectivity, and infrastructure readiness
Land & Materials — Land availability, potential land impacts including proximity to sensitive areas and ecosystems, and opportunities to reduce the embodied carbon of construction
Community & Economic Impact — Workforce availability, economic development opportunities, community impact, and proximity to users and other infrastructure
Your Objective
Find the world's most future-ready location for sustainable AI infrastructure.
Develop a data-driven decision framework that identifies where a next-generation AI data center should be located and demonstrates why that location outperforms the alternatives.
Your solution should balance environmental sustainability, infrastructure performance, resource efficiency, resilience, and economic viability—not optimize for a single metric.
Think beyond the building.
Think about the ecosystem.
OCP Relevance
Build the infrastructure of the future—before the future arrives.
OCP has demonstrated that data center infrastructure can be approached differently through open collaboration, innovation, efficiency, and scalable design.
Can we apply the same systems-thinking approach to where infrastructure is built?
Use open data, innovative technologies, and creative thinking to rethink the geography of AI infrastructure.
Don't just find a location. Design the decision framework that could change how the world builds data centers.
Deliverables
Teams should provide:
Recommended Geographic Location: city, region, or country
Decision Framework: Methodology and weighting of criteria
Data Analysis: Supporting datasets, assumptions, and insights
Sustainability Impact Assessment: Estimated carbon, water, and resource benefits
Risk Assessment:  Key environmental and operational risks
Implementation Vision: How the data center could operate sustainably over the next 20-30 years
