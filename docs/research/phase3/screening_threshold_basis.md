# screening_threshold_basis — documented bases for Phase 3 screening thresholds

Status: research evidence for Phase 3 (`docs/specs/phase3.md.txt`: "Thresholds must come from configuration with a stated rationale. Do not invent scientific or regulatory limits.")
Researched: 2026-10-02 (retrieval timestamps in the log are UTC and run into 2026-10-03). Researcher: research subagent (claude-sonnet-5-5) for the dc_locator technical lead.
Scope: six items — (1) flood, (2) protected areas / PAD-US GAP status, (3) wetlands / CWA Section 404, (4) wildfire (WHP, burn probability), (5) water stress (WRI Aqueduct), (6) land needs of ~100 MW data-center campuses.

## 0. How this report was produced, and how to read it

- Every number, class definition, and quoted phrase below was machine-extracted from a document that was actually downloaded and read in this session. Cached copies are in `data/raw/_literature/` (all files prefixed `stb_`); the retrieval log (URL, final URL, bytes, sha256, UTC time) is `data/raw/_literature/download_log_screening_threshold_basis.json`. The shared file `download_log.json` in that folder belongs to other agents and was not modified. Nothing here comes from memory.
- Retrieval method: `curl` with `User-Agent: dc_locator/0.1 (research prototype)`; no logins, forms, or CAPTCHAs; no browser impersonation. `fema.gov`, `federalregister.gov` (HTML), `congress.gov`, `justia.com`, `hines.com`, `cwcb.colorado.gov`, one USACE district site, and `hud.gov` refused or failed the plain request (HTTP 403/404 or an "unblock" page). Where that happened I used the same Federal Register / eCFR documents from `govinfo.gov` and the eCFR API, an Internet Archive snapshot of the FEMA page, or a mirror, and each such case is flagged in the source table (Appendix A).
- Quotes: United States federal government works are quoted as needed. For non-government sources (WRI, ASCE material, JLARC, arXiv, trade press) only short phrases are quoted and the rest is stated as facts with a location, to respect copyright.
- "Derived" = arithmetic done by this report on retrieved numbers (shown with the formula). Derived values are never presented as source values.
- Classification tags used throughout:
  - **REGULATORY** = binding legal text. Always check to whom and where it applies (many items below bind only federal agencies or only local permitting, not a private developer's siting choice).
  - **GUIDANCE** = non-binding recommendation, typical-practice statement, or standard excerpt.
  - **DEFINITION** = a dataset's or agency's own class/category scheme (it defines what a class means; it is not a limit).
  - **POLICY** = a decision the project must make and label `basis: project_policy` (or `project_assumption`); no retrieved document sets the value.
- Value "nature" follows the task request: measurement / survey statistic / model estimate / assumption, plus regulatory definition, guidance statement, classification convention where those fit better.
- UNKNOWN is a valid result: items I could not retrieve or verify are marked **UNVERIFIED** and are listed in section 9.

## 1. Summary matrix (what can be cited, and what is still a project decision)

Key takeaways:

- **No cutoff in this evidence set is a legal limit on a private data-center project's siting.** The regulatory texts found (EO 11988, 44 CFR Parts 9/59/60, CWA 404 and its regulations) bind federal agencies or set local/federal permitting steps; each screening threshold is therefore a `project_policy` (or `project_assumption`) that cites them as context.
- **Strongest data-center-specific basis (flood):** 44 CFR 9.4 names "data storage centers which contain records or services that may become lost or inoperative during flood and storm events" as a critical action, and 44 CFR 9.7(c)(2)(ii) requires the 0.2% annual-chance floodplain for critical actions. It binds FEMA actions only. FFRMS (EO 13690) is not in force.
- **PAD-US:** GAP 1-2 are the USGS "permanently protected and primarily managed for biodiversity conservation" classes and the classes USGS delivers to the World Database on Protected Areas; GAP codes are management-intent classes, not legal prohibitions.
- **Wetlands:** Section 404 context supports a precautionary screen (40 CFR 230.10(a)(3) presumption), but NLCD wetland classes are land-cover classes with a user's accuracy of 54% (NLCD 2019), not delineations. NWP 39 (2026) now names data centers and allows up to 1/2 acre of loss with notification.
- **Wildfire:** WHP classes are percentile bands (very high above the 95th percentile; high 84th-95th) of a relative index for fuels-treatment planning; no regulatory or published siting threshold was found.
- **Water stress:** the Aqueduct 4.0 categories (Low <10% ... Extremely high >80%) are WRI conventions; WRI calls Aqueduct "primarily a prioritization tool" with "limited added value on a local scale".
- **Land:** EPA's typical-practice statement is 100 acres for a 100 MW campus (0.4047 km^2, derived); documented figures differ by about an order of magnitude, and the MW basis (IT versus facility power) is a units trap.

### 2.3 What the evidence supports, and what it does not

- **No retrieved document makes the 1% floodplain a legal exclusion for a private data center.** EO 11988 and 44 CFR Part 9 bind federal agencies (EO11988 sec. 1; 44 CFR 9.2(a)). The NFIP rules (44 CFR 60.3) are local-permit design requirements. Using the SFHA (or 0.2% floodplain) as a screening exclusion is therefore **POLICY**, justified by (a) the federal policy of avoiding floodplains where a practicable alternative exists, (b) FEMA's own classification of data storage centers as a critical action, and (c) typical-practice statements. State and local floodplain rules exist and vary; none was retrieved (the Colorado CWCB rule and two other state/vendor pages were blocked, so state-level requirements are **UNVERIFIED**).
- **The strongest data-center-specific document basis for the 0.2% floodplain** is the combination of F3 + F4 (FEMA regulation naming data storage centers as critical actions and requiring the 0.2% floodplain for them) and F6/F7/F8 (federal guidance and FEMA advisory). Applicability to a non-federal project is by analogy only; the config rationale should say so.
- **Mapped-area limits matter for UNKNOWN handling.** FEMA itself states (F14) that regulatory mapping may not cover every area and that its own determinations use "best available information"; F9 shows substantial flood exposure outside the SFHA. A cell with no NFHL coverage must therefore be UNKNOWN for flood, never PASS (consistent with `AGENTS.md` rule 3). The retrieved FEMA text does not give a national percentage of mapped area (UNVERIFIED).
- **Scale mismatch.** The Phase 1 grid is 10 km x 10 km (`configs/grid.yaml`: `cell_size_m: 10000`) = 100 km^2 (derived: 10 km x 10 km). Floodplains are mapped as polygons far finer than that. Every document above defines the floodplain as an area, so a flood requirement is naturally an area-subtraction layer (suitable area after removing SFHA / 0.2% floodplain), not a cell-level pass/fail on "any intersection". That design choice is the project's.

### 3.3 Why GAP 1-2 are commonly treated as conservation lands, and what that does and does not mean

- USGS describes GAP 1 and 2 as "permanently protected and primarily managed for biodiversity conservation" (P6), and the USGS/WCMC/IUCN delivery convention selects only GAP 1 and 2 as protected areas for the World Database on Protected Areas (P8). That convention, not any statute, is the documented reason these two classes are used as "conservation lands".
- GAP 3 is defined as multiple-use land that allows extraction (logging, mining) and, per USGS, activities including "energy" (P3, P7). Treating GAP 3 as an automatic exclusion is therefore not supported by its definition; it is a possible **flag** (POLICY).
- GAP 4 explicitly includes "unknown areas" and "private lands" (P4); GAP 4 must never be read as "verified unprotected": PAD-US omits easements not suitable for public distribution and is not a legal record (P13, P14). A cell's absence from GAP 1-3 is "no known mandate", not PASS for a legal question (project rule: UNKNOWN never PASS).
- **GAP codes are not legal prohibitions** and not a measure of protection effectiveness (P9, P10). Excluding GAP 1-2 is **POLICY** that cites the USGS definition and the WDPA convention.
- Suggested config wording (proposal): `protected_area_exclusion: {gap_status: [1, 2], basis: project_policy, rationale: "USGS PAD-US GAP 1-2 = permanently protected and primarily managed for biodiversity (USGS 2021 technical announcement; USGS PAD-US Data Overview); also the classes USGS delivers to the WDPA. Management-intent classification, not a legal prohibition; PAD-US not authoritative for regulatory outcomes."}`; GAP 3 as `flag` (policy), GAP 4 as no exclusion.

### 4.3 What the evidence supports, and what it does not

- **Regulatory basis for avoiding wetlands in site selection (REGULATORY, context):** if a project needs to place fill in jurisdictional wetlands, the Guidelines presume that a practicable alternative without a special aquatic site exists for a non-water-dependent use such as a data center (W5), and the permit path is either an individual permit or NWP 39 up to 1/2 acre with pre-construction notification (W6). This supports a precautionary screening policy; it does not make any NLCD pixel a legal wetland.
- **NLCD is a satellite/ancillary-data land-cover classification, not a delineation (inference).** The retrieved NLCD documents define the wetland classes by vegetation fraction plus a "periodically saturated" substrate (W13, W14) and describe a modeled workflow (W15); the regulatory definition rests on soils, vegetation, and hydrology determined for the site, with jurisdiction further limited by "continuous surface connection" (W9, W12). No NLCD document that I retrieved says "not for jurisdictional use"; the closest explicit government statement is the FWS precaution for NWI (W17). The conclusion "NLCD wetlands are not jurisdictional delineations" is therefore an inference from W9-W17 and is marked **UNVERIFIED as an explicit NLCD statement**.
- **Accuracy is limited (W16).** For NLCD 2019, only 54% of the mapped area labelled wetland agreed with the primary reference label for either wetland class (derived: 100% - 54% = 46% disagreement), and producer's accuracy was 83% (woody) and 58% (herbaceous), so both false positives and missed wetlands are expected. These figures are for the 2019 product, not for Annual NLCD 2024.
- **Legal definition is moving (W10, W11).** The eCFR text in force on 2026-10-01 is the Sept. 2023 conforming rule; a proposal and a supplemental proposal are pending. Do not hard-code legal conclusions.
- **POLICY needed:** whether NLCD 90/95 is a hard exclusion (STRICT) and/or a flag (EXPLORATORY), and any buffer. Suggested wording (proposal): `wetland_screen: {layer: NLCD classes 90 and 95, basis: project_policy, rationale: "Precautionary screen: CWA 404(b)(1) presumes practicable alternatives exist for non-water-dependent uses (40 CFR 230.10(a)(3)); NLCD wetland classes are land-cover classes, not jurisdictional delineations (NLCD 2019 wetland user's accuracy 54%, Wickham et al. 2023, Table 4)."}`.

### 5.3 What the evidence supports, and what it does not

- **There is no regulatory limit.** I found no federal or national regulation that sets a WHP class or burn-probability value as a siting exclusion for a data center, and no published guidance giving a numeric cutoff for this use (two targeted web searches; the closest items were generic risk-framework papers and a vendor blog). Treat this as an absence of evidence in my searches, not proof (**UNVERIFIED negative**).
- **What the classes mean.** WHP classes are percentile bands of a relative index (WF5), designed for fuels-treatment prioritization (WF1, WF3, WF10). "High + very high" is a USFS-recognized grouping for areas "most likely to experience high-intensity wildfire" (WF6), covering about 12.6% of the 48-state + DC area (derived, WF7). A rule such as "FAIL if WHP is high or very high" would remove roughly that share of land before any other screen (derived), and would remove it on a relative-ranking basis, not on an absolute hazard basis.
- **Burn probability is a different quantity** (annual probability, 0-0.13 in the raster; WF11); USFS documents give no suggested cut values for it. It is one of the inputs from which WHP is built (WHP2023-META abstract: WHP is created from FSim annual burn probability and fire intensity datasets), so WHP and BP are not independent and must not be double counted in Phase 4.
- **Resolution.** WHP2023 is 270 m; BP is natively 270 m and only resampled to 30 m (WF12). Neither supports parcel-level claims (WF9, WF16). Cell-level use needs zonal statistics, not point sampling.
- **POLICY needed:** whether wildfire is a hard FAIL (STRICT), a flag, or only a Phase 4 metric. Suggested wording (proposal): `wildfire_screen: {layer: WHP 2023 classes 4-5 (high, very high), basis: project_policy, rationale: "USFS WHP classes are relative percentile bands (>84th percentile for high and very high); the USFS summary workbook notes that high plus very high is often used to represent areas most likely to experience high-intensity wildfire. No regulatory or published siting threshold exists; national-scale, not parcel-level (USFS WHP 2023 metadata; WRC FAQ)."}`.

### 6.3 What the evidence supports, and what it does not

- **The category names and break values are WRI conventions** (WS2, WS3), not regulatory limits and not physical thresholds, and WRI states that the indicators are not validated (WS6, WS10). A config that uses them must say "WRI Aqueduct 4.0 category" and cite p. 11 of the technical note; it must not call "Extremely high" a "legal" or "scientific" limit.
- **WRI's own appropriate-use statements limit what a screen may claim** (WS8, WS9): prioritization at large scale, followed by local deep dives. A screening result based on BWS is a relative water-stress flag for the sub-basin, not evidence about the availability of water for a specific 100 MW facility, not a water-supply commitment, and not about interbasin transfers or access (WS7, WS10). This matches the project rule "Aqueduct is not a confirmed water supply" (`AGENTS.md` sec. 3 rule 5).
- **"Arid and low water use" needs its own config line.** It is assigned score 5 (WS2) but is defined by two absolute thresholds on available water and withdrawal (WS4), not by the ratio; a config that maps categories by score alone would misread it.
- **Scale and boundaries.** Sub-basins (HydroBASINS level 6) are far coarser than a parcel and the break-value ownership (lower versus upper class) is not specified (WS2); a config should state its convention explicitly (for example "category 4 means raw value above 80%").
- **POLICY needed:** which category (if any) is a FAIL in STRICT, which is a flag in EXPLORATORY, and how arid/low-water-use basins are handled. Suggested wording (proposal): `water_stress_screen: {source: WRI Aqueduct 4.0 baseline water stress, flag_categories: [extremely_high, arid_and_low_water_use], basis: project_policy, rationale: "WRI category 'Extremely high' = more than 80% of available renewable supply withdrawn (Technical Note p. 11); WRI states Aqueduct is a prioritization tool with limited added value at local scale and that indicators are not validated (pp. 12, 36). ### 7.3 What the evidence supports, and what it does not

- **The only government figure that ties acres to MW for a 100 MW campus is EPA's L1**, and EPA cites no source for it. It is a typical-practice statement in guidance written for reuse of contaminated land, not a requirement and not a measurement.
- **The documented range is wide (derived comparison):** EPA 100 acres per 100 MW = 1.0 acre per MW (L1); the Virginia fleet average is about 1.43 acres per MW (L12, facility MW); DOE site proposals list 110 acres for 1,000 MW at Argonne, about 0.11 acre per MW (L7; derived 110/1,000). The values differ by about an order of magnitude because they measure different things (campus-with-expansion versus a developable pad; facility power versus IT power; fleet average versus design target). A single `minimum_land_area_km2` is therefore a **POLICY / project assumption**, to be labelled `basis: project_assumption` with this range cited as context.
- **Units trap:** EPA, EO 14318, CRS, JLARC and the preprint speak of facility or "new load" MW (JLARC and the preprint say explicitly that cooling and overhead are included); the project's `peak_it_power_mw` is IT power. Facility MW = IT MW x PUE, and PUE is a separate assumption in Phase 3. Do not apply "100 acres per 100 MW" to IT MW without stating that.
- **Candidate value for the config (proposal, not a source value):** 100 acres = 0.4046873 km^2 (L17) if the project adopts EPA's typical-practice minimum; any smaller value would rest on DOE site proposals (L7-L9), and any larger value on L12 or L18.
- **Contiguity (spec requirement).** EPA's own checklist asks for contiguous parcels and for "how much of the site is developable" (L4), which matches the Phase 3 requirement to keep (a) potentially suitable total area, (b) largest contiguous suitable area, and (c) confirmed developable parcel area separate. The 10 km grid cell is 100 km^2 (derived), about 247 times the 0.405 km^2 figure (derived: 100 km^2 / 0.4046873 km^2), so the land-area requirement is only meaningful on contiguous patches derived from the 30 m NLCD raster; if contiguity cannot be established, the requirement stays UNKNOWN. Slope, if used, can cite L3 (typical-practice statement only).
- **Other items in the EPA criteria table** (power, water, fiber, flood) are consistent with the Phase 3 requirement groups but are typical-practice statements; for example EPA lists "millions of gallons per MW per year" for cooling water and a 1-10 mile distance to a municipal water supply, neither of which is a requirement.


## 8. Cross-cutting implications for the Phase 3 configuration (proposals for the technical lead)

Everything in this section is a proposal that follows from sections 2-7; none of it is a source statement.

1. **Give every threshold a `basis` and an `applies_to`.** Suggested enum: `regulatory` (only if the text binds the project's own action), `guidance`, `definition`, `project_policy`, `project_assumption`. In this evidence set, **no screening cutoff qualifies as `regulatory` for a private data-center project.** The regulatory texts (EO 11988, 44 CFR Part 9, NFIP 44 CFR 59/60, CWA 404) bind federal agencies or local/federal permitting steps, and are cited as *context for a policy choice*.
2. **Carry citations as tag + location** (for example `44 CFR 9.7(c)(2)(ii)`, `WHP2023 metadata p. 12`, `Aqueduct 4.0 Technical Note p. 11`) plus an `as_of` date for every regulatory citation, because the FFRMS, the waters-of-the-U.S. definition, and the nationwide permits changed between 2021 and 2026 (sections 2.5 and 4).
3. **Use area subtraction for polygon-defined exclusions** (SFHA / 0.2% floodplain, PAD-US GAP 1-2, NLCD wetlands) and **zonal statistics for continuous or class rasters** (WHP 270 m, burn probability 270 m native, Aqueduct sub-basin polygons). Do not test "any intersection with the 100 km^2 cell".
4. **UNKNOWN rules that the sources support.** Flood: no NFHL coverage means UNKNOWN (FEMA says mapping may not cover all areas, F14). PAD-US: absence from GAP 1-3 is "no known mandate" and not proof of no protection (P4, P13, P14). Wetlands: NLCD accuracy is limited (W16); keep confidence `low` or `medium`, not `high`. Wildfire: WHP classes 6 (non-burnable) and 7 (open water) are separate classes, not "very low hazard" (WF4); map them to their own status rather than to the lowest-hazard class. Water: "arid and low water use" is separate from the ratio categories (WS2, WS4).
5. **Never present these as scientific or regulatory limits.** Use wording such as "screening policy referencing X" and keep the AGENTS.md section 8 interpretation sentence in every report.
